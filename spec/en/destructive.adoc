= Destructive actions
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

These rules cover actions that delete data or end something, such as deleting a record or closing an account.
They keep a person from losing work by mistake.

== Undo first

[[STY-040]]
*STY-040.* When a product can reverse an action, it SHOULD act at once and offer an undo.
It SHOULD NOT ask the person to confirm first.
The undo MUST stay available for at least as long as the message that offers it.
A question before every action trains people to confirm without reading.
An undo costs nothing when the action was right.

== Confirmation

[[STY-041]]
*STY-041.* An action that cannot be undone MUST ask the person to confirm it first, in a dialog.
The dialog MUST name the object and the result, such as "Delete asset 4521? You will lose its history."
The confirming button MUST repeat the verb of the action, such as "Delete asset".
It MUST NOT read "OK" or "Yes".
A specific label tells the person what will happen without reading the rest of the dialog.

[[STY-042]]
*STY-042.* The confirming button of a destructive action MUST use the danger tokens.
When the dialog opens, focus MUST go to the least destructive action, such as "Cancel".
The Escape key and the cancel button MUST close the dialog without acting.
This follows the dialog pattern of the link:https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/[Authoring Practices Guide] of the {abbr-w3c-first}.
A person who presses Enter by habit then cancels instead of deleting.

[[STY-043]]
*STY-043.* For a severe action, a dialog MAY ask the person to type the name of the object.
Severe results include the loss of a whole account or of many records.
The confirming button then MUST stay disabled until the typed name matches.
The dialog MUST say what to type, as STY-024 asks for any disabled control.
A typed name makes the person stop and read.
It also slows every use, so a product keeps it for rare and severe actions.

[[STY-044]]
*STY-044.* A destructive action MUST start from a button that submits a form, never from a plain link.
A browser, a search robot or a preview tool can follow a link without any person asking.
